These are photos of samples of most of the parts used in the Michelson interferometer kits. Not all parts will be identical to what's shown but they should be close enough to get you started.

But first are annotated photos of the V2.1 setups for both Homodyne and Heterodyne with all the optics on the PBS cube for reference. V2.0 is virtually identical except for the height of the optical centerline. V1.0 and V1.5 (homodyne only) will be generally similar except primarily for the custom un-anodized breadboard and classy wood block PBS mount. But this should serve for parts identification. Click on the relevant annotated graphic for a high resolution view displayed in a single new window or tab depending on your browser's settings.

  • PZT actuator parts: The PieZo Transducer moves order of a micron with the application of 10 V or so.

    PZT, Mirror, and Washer

  • Loudspeaker voice coil actuator parts: The loudspeaker should move enough to be detected by the interferometer (but not the human eye) with as little as a mA or less of current.

  • Hardware - Caphead screws, setscrews, washers: These are typical of the types of fasteners in the kit. Setscrews are just short threaded rods with a hex "slot" at one end. Caphead screws have a real head ;-). In both cases the length is of the threaded part only.

    From Left to Right:4-40 setscrews, #4 washer, 4-40 caphead screws, 6-32 caphead screws, 8-32 setscrew, 8-32 caphead screws, #10 washer, 1/4-20 setscrew, 1/4-20 caphead screws

    Note: M3 caphead screws are similar in diameter but will NOT fit 4-40 threaded holes and vice-versa. Don't force either!

  • Resistors: Resistors do not have polarity. There are 4 types used here:

    From Left to Right: Fixed Resistors, Power Resistor, Trim-pots, Potentiometer with Knob

    1. Low power fixed resistors: These do not have a polarity and are identified by color bands on their body:


      Resistor Color Code Chart (from the Digikey Web site)

      The ones used mostly here will look similar to the top example with the 4 band code and are rated at 1/4 watt with a body around 1/4 inch long. Their leads may be trimmed to conveniently fit the solderless breadboard (or PCB if used).

    2. Power resistors: Used in the Thermal Expansion experiment. They are white ceramic with the ratings labled on one side. The ones used here are 25 ohms, 5 watts.

    3. Trim-pots: Small potentiometer with screwdriver adjustment. Used here for signal level and threshold for the various biased photodiode detectors and interface to µMD0. They are labeled "10x" on one side where the "x" is the multiplier. For example "103" would be 10K or 10,000 ohms.

    4. Potentiometer with knob: Used to adjust the current to the loud speaker or voltage to the PZT. There will probably be a 10K ohm resistor (coverted with heatshink) in series with the wiper for protection since very little current is required to move the loudspeaker cone by a detectable amount. And essentially non current for the PZT.
