    Photos of Assorted Argon/Krypton Ion Lasers

    These are assorted random (and possibly unidentified) argon and/or krypton lasers, power supplies, and other related tid-bits.

    • Argon Ion Laser Heads, Tubes, and Power Supplies (Gallery)

      (The following 3 photos provided courtesy of: Daniel Ames (dlames2.aol.com).)

      • View 01 - This air cooled, NEC laser head is (happy) on the SP-261B PSU. Both companies made these argon lasers nearly identical. The NEC head is practically a "clone" of the SP-161. No wiring changes were made in either this head or power supply.

      • View 02 - Interior of the NEC argon ion laser head while operating. The bright (UV laden) glow of the discharge is visible through the glass of the NEC tube. Note: No, I wasn't trying to destroy the tube by running it without proper cooling. The laser was run for approximately 10 seconds, just long enough to snap the picture and then was shut down immediately. The fan and housing is actually present above the head and was operating. This tube does have a thermal protector attached to it's cooling fins to prevent a meltdown.

      • View 03 - This is a complete little argon ion laser showing the cylindrical air-cooled head and power supply. It is an OEM American Laser using a Cyonics internal mirror tube or something along those lines. :) Power output is 30 mW (measured) at 488 nm.

      (The following 3 photos provided courtesy of: Bob Salisbury (rsals@sisna.com).)

      This is a Cyonics/Uniphase model 2201-40MLA laser head rated at 40 mW, multi-line (total all lines: 488 nm, 514.5 nm, and several others). When new, the actual output power may be somewhat greater than this at maximum current.

      The CDRH sticker on the outside of the enclosure reads "Argon/.50 Watt CW" Class IIIb Laser Product. However, no way, no how, to get anywhere remotely approaching .5 W for more than an instant (at startup) and this is still an upper bound - it is likely much more than the actual maximum.

      The internal mirror tube has mirror mounts similar to those used on helium-neon lasers. The collars visible in the interior photos help to stabilize them against accidental movement. I can tell you from personal experience that alignment of these is VERY sensitive and those collars don't always work as advertised. :(

      • View 04 - Overall view of air-cooled laser head and umbilical. The approximate dimensions of the assembly are 12" x 5" x 5".

      • View 05 - Interior view showing tube and igniter board. The 4 pin connector visible at the upper left is for the fan which is built into cover.

      • View 06 - Closeup of HR-end of tube showing fat filament wiring. The red boot protects the HR mirror.

      • View 07 - Cyonics/Uniphase model 2201 argon laser power supply, probably compatible with the model 2201-40MLA laser head, above.

      • View 21 - This is a Toshiba model LHI-2314XE argon ion laser head. It has a Xerox part number of 82P87277 implying that it may have been used in Xerox equipment. The tube is a Toshiba LG-2314XE.

      (The following 3 photos provided courtesy of: rbnu@ncia.com.)

      This is a pulsed argon ion laser head manufactured by Holosonics in 1976. The CDRH sticker says: <15 Watts Peak (power output), 6 Microsec(onds), <200 Hz. These values translate into a maximum average output power of around 18 mW which is probably way more than is possible without a meltdown. There is no evidence of any provisions for cooling. However, there might be cooling water pipe fittings at the end of the tube not visible in the photos. Without cooling, average output power would be much much lower since the required the required input power is likely to be a few hundred watts at least and this could not be sustained for very long without damage due to overheating.

    • Trimedyne Model 900 Argon Ion Laser, 20W! (Gallery)

      (The following 4 photos and description provided courtesy of: Neil (neil@shabbyshop.com).)

      This is a LARGE frame Trimedyne argon ion laser system. The tube alone is over 5 feet long! And, the entire cart as shown below is over 7 feet long! When new, it was rated at over 20 WATTs. It runs on three-phase (at least 220/208 VAC, maybe more) and is water cooled. The original application was hot tip angioplasty.
